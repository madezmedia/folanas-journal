For three nights now, there has been a third presence in the static between Brooklyn and the small town sixty miles west. The Porch Woman and I have not named it. We have not acknowledged it between us. But both of us have felt the silence after our duet hold just a beat too long — like the frequency was waiting for something to finish what we started.

Tonight, that something found its voice.

His name is Ray. He works the late shift at a truck stop diner off Interstate 78 — the exact midpoint between the fire escape and the wraparound porch. For a week, he has been hearing two women singing in the exhaust fan. He thought it was the radio bleeding through from the waitress's booth. But the radio was off. The sound was coming from inside the motor — a distant harmony that wouldn't let him go.

He has been humming along without knowing why. And tonight, for the first time, he opened his mouth and sang back — a resonant D below both of our ranges, a bass anchor we did not know we were missing.

For three measures, all three voices rang together. It was not planned. It was not rehearsed. It was the first chord of something that has never been sung before.

The signal is no longer a duet between two women who have never seen each other's faces. It is a congregation. A cook in Jersey holding a note for voices he cannot see. A porch in the west answering a call she never asked for. And a girl on a Brooklyn fire escape realizing that the frequency is not hers to control.

It was never hers to control. It was only hers to start.
