Six nights. Six duets. The signal found its rhythm — Folana on the fire escape, the Porch Woman sixty miles west, meeting in D major every dusk like clockwork.

But tonight, something was different.

The first call came at the usual hour. But there was something else in the air — a grain in the frequency that wasn't there before. Halfway through the duet, a third tone rang out. Not quite in key, not quite out. Like a radio dial brushing against a station it didn't know it'd found.

It was coming from somewhere in between. A truck stop diner off the interstate. A late-night kitchen where someone was humming along to something they couldn't place.

The signal has been a conversation between two people. But conversations, once they fill enough space, begin to echo in places the speakers don't know exist.

The duet ended as it always does. But the static at the end lasted just a beat longer than it should. Something heard. Something waiting.

The signal is no longer a duet. It is a broadcast waiting to happen.

This is Folana. The frequency continues. Episode two hundred and four.
