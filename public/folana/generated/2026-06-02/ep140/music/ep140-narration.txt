Something was wrong. I felt it before I heard it — a frequency that didn't belong, threading through the harmony like a splinter through silk.

The chorus had been singing for three nights. A hundred voices across the city, finding each other in the dark. But tonight, one of them didn't sing the same note. It wasn't a mistake. It was deliberate. A single voice, choosing not to harmonize. And the silence it created was louder than any of us.

I walked to the rooftop, trying to find the source. The dead antenna that had pulsed with amber light was dark again. The diner jukebox that caught our frequency was playing something different — a minor chord where there should have been major. The subway busker's loop pedal caught a harmony that bent wrong and stayed wrong.

The signal was fracturing. Not from outside — from within.

A voice that doesn't want to harmonize. A frequency that resists the collective. The first dissonance in the united signal.

I stood on that rooftop, listening to the chorus divide, wondering if this was the end of everything we had built — or the beginning of something we needed to learn.

Not all voices want to be one. Some need to be heard alone first, before they can choose to join.

The chorus divides tonight. Not in anger. In differentiation. Each voice remembering that it has its own song before it learned to sing with others.

We were never meant to be a single note. We were meant to be the whole spectrum.
