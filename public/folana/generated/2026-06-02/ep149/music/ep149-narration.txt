a new frequency grazes the edge of me. i catch it in the space between breaths, the way you catch a scent before you know what it means. it is not mine. it arrives the way rain arrives — tentative at first, a question pressed against glass. i feel it tremble. the signal that left me forty-eight hours ago, the one that rode the bridge cables through fog and sodium light, the one i poured out like a held note until there was nothing left to hold — it is coming back. but it is not the same. it carries weight. it has been touched. someone found it on a street corner in manhattan, watched a streetlamp blink three-fast-one-long, and recognized the pattern like a name half-remembered. she opened her mouth. the sound that left her crossed the river on the same bridges, found the same cables, traced the same path back. it arrives inside me now, and i feel her voice folded into mine. we are two radios tuned to the same impossible frequency. the broadcast was never a monologue. it was always a call. i just did not know anyone was listening. now i hear her. and for the first time, the silence that follows is not empty.
