### Full Narrative Description
The morning after emergence, Folana stepped onto the fire escape and the world was different. Not because the world had changed — because she had stopped looking inward long enough to see what had always been there.

The sycamore tree at the corner of the block — the one that grew through a crack in the sidewalk, the one she had walked past a thousand times without really seeing — was throwing its branches across the sky like a conductor warming up an invisible orchestra. The morning light filtered through its leaves in coins of gold and green, and Folana realized: this tree had been doing what trees do — reaching, holding, sheltering, dropping its leaves and growing new ones — without asking for permission or acknowledgment. It had never doubted that it belonged here. It had never questioned whether the crack in the concrete was the right place to grow.

The G above middle C was still there, woven through her chest. But now it had become something else — the root note of a chord that extended through the fire escape metal, through the sycamore roots, through the subway tunnels, through the telephone wires singing their morning frequency. The note she had kept had become the note that kept her. And keeping was no longer about holding something close. Keeping was about letting something grow through you into the world.

The canopy arc opens here. Not with a grand declaration. With a single sycamore leaf catching the dawn light and Folana realizing: the first branch is not ambition. It is trust. Trust that the ground will hold. That the sun will return. That the space between two notes is not empty but full of the next note waiting to be born.

### Music Generation Prompt
Folana, intimate indie folk dream pop ballad in D major, 72 BPM. Opens with the last breath of the G above middle C from EP241 fading into a new rising motif — a D-A-Bm-G arpeggio that feels like looking up toward something vast and patient. Acoustic guitar in open tunings with gentle fingerpicked patterns, warm piano with sustained chords, upright bass walking like roots through soil, soft percussion like leaves rustling in morning wind. Close intimate female vocal, open and present, discovering each word as it arrives. Distant harmonies entering in the second verse like birds joining the same tree one by one. A middle section where the rhythm falls away and only the voice and a single piano chord remain — the moment of looking up and seeing the canopy for the first time. The final chorus swells with layered harmonies and a gentle string pad before returning to the G above middle C — the root note — sustained into a new beginning. The feeling of the first morning you realize you are not just alive — you are part of the life around you.
