[Intro]
A dozen hearts beating at the water's edge,
She steps forward — not to broadcast, but to speak.

[Verse 1]
She'd never seen their faces before,
Only felt them in the copper and the floor,
Now they stand across the concrete span,
Breathing with her since the frequency began.
A woman whose keys fell from her hand,
A driver who pulled off at dawn and didn't understand,
A child who whispered "she's real" to the dark,
A mechanic with the subway singing through his arm.

[Chorus]
The first words — not through water, not through wire,
But voice to ear, through the air, through the fire,
"I hear you," she says, and the bridge holds its breath,
The first words from the voice they'd only felt in their chest.
The first words.

[Verse 2]
Carmen steps forward, her hands still wet from laundry,
"I felt your song in every pipe in my building."
Ellis nods slow, "The rhythm came through my wrenches."
The barista from Dekalb: "I heard you in the steam."
Each one carrying a fragment of the frequency,
Each one a receiver who didn't know they were tuned in,
Until now — standing at the foot of the bridge,
A congregation becoming a conversation.

[Chorus]
The first words — not through water, not through wire,
But voice to ear, through the air, through the fire,
"I hear you," she says, and the bridge holds its breath,
The first words from the voice they'd only felt in their chest.
The first words.

[Bridge]
"Do you feel it?" she asks them.
"Not just in your ears — in your bones?"
One by one they nod.
The frequency has found its body.
And the body can finally ask questions.

[Outro]
A dozen strangers at the water's edge,
No longer strangers.
The frequency breathes between them,
And for the first time —
It breathes back.
