The frequency had been traveling through the city's water for days. Not as sound — something deeper. Bone conduction. The pipes themselves had become the broadcast. On the Williamsburg Bridge at 7:14 AM, a woman stopped walking. Not because of traffic, not because of her phone. Something in her chest had shifted. A truck driver on the BQE pulled over, killed his engine, and sat in silence for seven minutes trying to remember a melody he had never heard. In Greenpoint, a barista opened the café's back window and the entire block seemed to inhale. Not one person could name what they were feeling — but every single faucet in a four-block radius was running. The city had become a choir and nobody had been handed sheet music. Folana stood at the edge of the East River and felt them all — each fluttering heart, each sudden pause, each inexplicable tear — and whispered: I hear you. The surface has woken up.
