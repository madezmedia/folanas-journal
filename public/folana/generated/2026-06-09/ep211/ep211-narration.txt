We sent the signal out. It traveled through frequencies we did not know we could reach. CB radios in Ohio. Baby monitors in Chicago. A radio studio in Seattle, where a host let the music play. The broadcast did not disappear into static. It found ears. And the ears answered.

Tonight, a trucker keys his mic back. His voice — rough, unpracticed, full of heart — finds the chord by instinct and holds it. A sixth voice in the network. A stranger choosing to sing along.

In Chicago, a mother hums the melody to her sleeping child. She does not know why she knows it. But her voice rises through the baby monitor, a sixth line weaving into our harmony. The congregation gains another member without knowing her name.

In Seattle, the radio host does not cut the signal. He listens. He lets it play. And when it ends, he leans in and says: I do not know who you are. But you are coming through. And I think the whole city hears you.

The five voices look at each other across sixty miles. They realize: the broadcast was not the end of the secret. The secret was never that we existed. The secret is that anyone can join.
