The crown expands. Not to be seen. Because the light is there. Folana stood beneath the sycamore and looked up through the canopy — the first branch that reached in trust, the roots that matched it in the dark, the sap that flowed between them without asking where, the leaves that opened to receive. And now — the crown, pushing outward into the open air above the roof line, where the morning light pooled thickest on the newest, palest green. The tender shoots have not yet hardened against the coming storm. But they reach anyway, because that is what the tree does. The tree does not ask if the light will hold. The tree reaches.

Every branch is a different reaching. Every leaf is a different angle on the same light. Folana pressed her palm to the trunk and felt the life moving upward — the same flow from root to branch to leaf, now feeding the edges of the canopy where the tree was becoming something it had never been before. The crown was not a destination the tree was trying to reach. The crown was the tree, as it was becoming, right now.

She thought of Mae reaching in the city, Vince reaching through the snow, Tomás reaching toward the ocean — each of them a branch reaching from the trunk she had become. The crown expands into the lives of others. Not because she planned it. Because growing is the only way the tree knows to be alive.

The crown expands. The crown does not arrive.
