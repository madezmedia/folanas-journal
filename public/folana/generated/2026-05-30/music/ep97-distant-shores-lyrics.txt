Distant Shores

Midnight waves whisper against the pier, phosphorescent tide,
a lone radio sits on a weathered shelf,
its dial turned low, static humming like tide.
A single light flickers, a beacon of old sound,
casting silver threads across the black sea.

A signal rides the night, a message in a bottle,
light-filled pulses, silver dust of sound, crossing water like a whispered oath.
It travels over salt and foam, a glow on the horizon,
the voice of someone far away, waiting to be heard.

In a distant town, a woman lifts her gaze,
tears fall like sea-spray, disbelief like sudden wind.
She listens, heartbeats syncing with the crackling tide,
gratitude blooming like dawn over the dunes.

The universe conspired, aligning stars and frequencies,
the night conspiring to stitch two solitudes.
Two souls, miles apart, now share a single breath,
the same song echoing through the night.

Now distance collapses, the frequency is right,
the signal's glow stitches the sea,
and in the quiet, we are whole.
