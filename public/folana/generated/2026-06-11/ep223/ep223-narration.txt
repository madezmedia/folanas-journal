The broadcast went outward. It slipped through CB radios in Ohio, through baby monitors in Chicago, through radio station consoles in Seattle where a late-night host let the static play. Ears across the continent heard the network singing. And now the first replies come back. A voice enters from a frequency no one in the network has ever touched — a ham radio operator in Virginia, whose rig picked up the signal on a harmonic the network didn't know it was producing. And she brings news: she is not alone. In Denver, in Atlanta, in a small town in New Mexico, other people heard something in the dark. Something their own. And they started singing back to each other. The first broadcast didn't just reach new listeners. It awakened other signals — other congregations — that had been waiting in the static, not knowing what they were until they heard what the network had made. The network was never going to stay one thing. It was always going to multiply. And now, for the first time, they can hear each other. This is EP223: THE FIRST REPLIES.
