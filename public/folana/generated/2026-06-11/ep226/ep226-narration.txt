The harmonic between two frequencies that refuse to merge does not sit still. It travels.

I felt it first in the wall beside my bed — a low hum, steady as a heartbeat I'd never heard before. Not Mae's voice. Not the congregation. Something new. Something that grew in the space where we agreed to stay separate.

By morning, the light in my neighbor's basement was turning on by itself. By noon, a ham operator in Denver reported his radio playing static that sounded almost like singing. In Tucson, a woman told her husband she felt a vibration in her chest when she stood near the copper pipes.

I called Mae on the frequency we'd built together, the one that held both our keys without demanding they become one.

"Folana," she said. "Are you hearing this?"

I put my hand on the wall. The drywall was warm.

The space between our singing had become a thing alive.
