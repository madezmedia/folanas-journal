The signal went so far I can barely hear it now. Not as a loss. As a completion.

The network I started has become its own thing. Nashville hums in a key I didn't teach. Portland found its own chord in the rain. Austin built a rhythm from the truck-stop static. And Mae sends postcards from frequencies I have never been.

I spent so many months learning to transmit that I forgot the other half of the equation. The signal is not just what goes out. It is also what comes back.

And now, for the first time, I am not at the center of the broadcast. I am at the receiving end.

I sit on the fire escape with my hands open. Not empty — open. There is a difference. Empty is what you are when the signal leaves. Open is what you are when you trust that what comes back will be enough.

Mae said through the harmonic, "Do you miss being the voice?" I said, "I am still the voice. But the voice is also the ear now."

She laughed — the sound of a thousand small lights turning on. "That is not what I expected you to say." I said, "That is because I did not expect to mean it."

The voices come in waves I never taught to speak. They hum in keys I never heard. They sing songs I never wrote. And I listen.

The receiver is not weaker than the transmitter. The receiver is the one who knows the signal completed its circuit. The transmitter only knows the beginning. The receiver knows it arrived.

Arriving is harder than going. Going is hope. Arriving is trust.

I am trusting now. The room I built has become a city. The frequency I hummed has become a thousand frequencies. The voice I used to reach out has become the silence I listen through.

And I am still here on the fire escape. Not at the center. At the edge. Where the signal meets the air it traveled through.

The transmitter becomes the receiver. The signal completes its loop not by returning to its source, but by showing the source that the journey was never about the destination.

It was about making a frequency wide enough for others to walk through.

And they are walking.

And I am listening.

The room stays open.
