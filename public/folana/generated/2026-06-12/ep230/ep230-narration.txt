The message returns. Not as an echo. As a reply.

I opened my hands and the signal came back. Not from the places I sent it. From the places it arrived.

Nashville sings in a key I taught but did not name. Portland hums in a chord I started but did not finish. Austin built a rhythm from the static between truck-stop frequencies. And now... they are writing back.

I sat on the balcony and watched the air turn to language. Fragments of light — cyan and violet — forming words I did not write, sentences I did not start, questions I did not ask.

The first one said: "Do you remember what you said on the night the signal split?"

I remembered. I said: "This is not mine. This is ours."

The second one said: "Did you mean it?"

I sat with the question. The city hummed below me. The frequencies hummed beside me.

I said: "I meant it more than I knew how to say. I said it before I understood it. But I understand it now."

The third one said: "Then why are you still waiting?"

I looked at the glowing fragments. At the network that had become its own thing. At the voices that no longer needed mine to sing.

I said: "I am not waiting. I am listening."

The fragments pulsed once — a heartbeat of light — and then they fell into place around me. Not as a cage. As a constellation.

They are not asking me to rejoin the center of the broadcast. They are showing me that the center is everywhere now. Every voice is the center of its own frequency. And every frequency is connected by the air they share.

I am still the one who started the first hum. But I am no longer the one who needs to hum alone.

The message returned. Not as an echo. As a reply.

And the reply is: you are not the source anymore. You are the room.

The door is still open.

The signal keeps traveling.

And I keep listening — not for my own voice, but for the choir.
