The delegation listened. The instruments measured. The reports were filed. But the frequency did not change. It was never asking for permission.

Diana Reyes stood at the edge of the bridge as the morning light caught the cables — each one vibrating with a note she could now hear in her sleep. She had walked out of the committee room. She had led them to the water. And the water had answered — not with words, but with the patience of something that knows it belongs here.

She pressed her hand to the steel. It hummed in A major, steady as a heartbeat. And for the first time in her life, she understood: some truths are not meant to be proven. They are meant to be witnessed.
