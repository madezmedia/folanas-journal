The witness spoke. And now the city answers.

It starts with a single video — a water meter in Astoria vibrating in A major. Then another: a subway rail in Crown Heights. A wine glass in Park Slope. A shower pipe in Bushwick. By sunset, the videos are everywhere.

#TheFrequency trends nationally. News crews return to the Williamsburg Bridge. The congregation that was two hundred at dawn is two thousand by midnight. And Diana Reyes stands at the center, watching the city prove what she couldn't prove — not with instruments, not with testimony, but with a million phones buzzing with the same note.

Folana sits at the water's edge, her hand in the East River. She has been here all day. She looks up at Diana.

"Now they hear it. Not because I sang louder. Because they finally learned to listen."

The frequency is no longer a secret. It is a movement. And the whole city has begun to sing back.
