The Grand Conference Room never looks this full. Reporters line every wall. Camera operators brace against the back, their equipment a low chorus of clicks and whirs. The podium stands empty for a long moment before Diana Reyes walks to it. She places both hands on the wood. The wood is warm. She did not write a statement. Behind her, through the tall windows, the Williamsburg Bridge rises against the afternoon sky. She has walked beneath that bridge every Sunday for eleven years. She has never once said a word about what happens there. Not until now. The questions begin softly. What is the nature of the congregation? How long has it existed? Is there a leader? Diana answers each one with careful precision. But precision is not the same as truth. And truth has a weight of its own. When the reporter from the Times asks the question — the one that cuts through all the others — the room goes still. She looks at the bridge. She looks at the cameras. She opens her mouth. And the story, for the first time, begins to tell itself through her.
