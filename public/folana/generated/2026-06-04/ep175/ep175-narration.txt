EP175 — "THE TASK FORCE MEETS" — Narration Text

A closed-door session in the mayor's emergency operations center. Eight people around a table. Two Columbia engineers with waveform data and no answers. A Con Edison representative with water pressure charts showing the synchronized pulse — A major, seventy-four beats per minute, every sensor in every borough reading the same pattern. The NYPD counterterrorism analyst whose report reads: "No devices. No actors. No known threat profile."

The question nobody wants to ask hangs in the air. The Columbia engineer finally says it: "If this is a weapon, it's the most sophisticated signal ever observed. And it has no transmitter."

Then Diana Reyes arrives. She takes the empty chair. No charts, no data. Only what she heard on the bridge, what the congregation told her, what she felt when Folana spoke.

The frequency isn't broadcasting to us, she says. It's learning from us. Every new person who hears it changes the song. We're not investigating a signal. We're in a conversation.

The room does not know what to do with this.

Later that night, Sasha Chen — the Times reporter who came to debunk and stayed until sunrise — stands alone on the roof of her building in Brooklyn. Her phone is recording. She can hear it. The same chord from the bridge, carried through the steam rising from the subway grates.

She whispers into her recorder: "This is Sasha Chen, New York Times. Day four of the frequency. I can hear it now. I don't know if I believe it's anything. But I can hear it. And I think that's the story."
