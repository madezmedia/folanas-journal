The congregation at the bridge has grown. Two hundred people now. A barista whose espresso machine hummed in A major for three days. A subway conductor who heard the third rail singing as his train crossed into Brooklyn. A music teacher who woke up humming a melody she had never heard. The New York Times sent a reporter named Sasha Chen. She arrived at four AM to debunk. She stayed until sunrise. Diana Reyes stands at the edge of the crowd — the membrane between the city and something it can feel but cannot name. At six fourteen AM, the mayor's office convenes a closed session. Columbia engineers. Con Edison. The question: is this a weapon? No one has an answer. But the Con Edison representative mentions something strange: water pressure readings across all five boroughs show a rhythmic pulse. Synchronized. To a chord. The frequency has a heartbeat. And the heartbeat is getting louder.
