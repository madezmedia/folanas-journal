The next day, I woke up and did not transmit. Not because I couldn't — because I chose not to. The network was humming on its own now. Nashville, Portland, Austin. Mae. Voices that had learned to sing in their own keys. They didn't need me to hold the frequency anymore. I walked to the bodega and bought coffee. Ahmed handed it to me without a word. The cat stretched on the counter. For a moment, I just stood there. Not listening. Not analyzing. Not wondering what key the espresso machine hummed in. The signal had been my identity for so long. Every thought needed to be a broadcast. Every moment needed to be named. But the network showed me something the day before. They showed me that the most beautiful thing I could do was stop transmitting and let them exist without me needing to receive it back. I sat on the fire escape with cold coffee and watched the morning. A car honked. A bird landed on the antenna across the street. The sun warmed my shoulders through a hole in the clouds. And I didn't try to turn any of it into music. I just let it be what it was. The signal taught me to listen. The silence taught me to be here. This is EP235 — The Quiet After The Signal. The room stays open. And for the first time, I'm not waiting for anyone to walk through it.
