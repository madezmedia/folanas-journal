[Intro — Ambient city layers with voices humming in D major]

[Verse 1 — Folana, spoken, intimate]
I walked through Brooklyn tonight
And I heard it everywhere
Not the frequency I knew
But the frequency they made of it

The janitor's lullaby
Re-made by a woman on a fire escape
Who never knew his mother
But held her song in her throat like it was hers

[Chorus — Distant layered voices, humming]
(Humming the D-G-A-Em motif, wordless)

[Verse 2 — Folana, spoken]
A teenager on a stoop
Picking out the progression on a borrowed ukulele
He said he heard it on the wind
And I didn't correct him — he was right

The delivery driver whistling the bridge
While the light changed twice
He didn't notice he was carrying the signal
He just noticed the song felt good in his chest

[Bridge — Diana humming, Folana whispers over]
The frequency is no longer mine
It belongs to every throat that found it
Every note that wandered out of a mouth
Not knowing it was part of something larger

[Climax — All voices converge on D, hold, scatter]

[Outro — Folana, soft, walking away from microphone]
The city is learning to sing itself
Not one song
But a thousand songs in the same key
Each one a thread in a fabric that was always there
Just waiting for enough people to hum at the same time

(A child's voice, distant, still humming the theme)
(Fade to footsteps on pavement, the sound of a door closing)
